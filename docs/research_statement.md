# Research Statement

## Background

I am a legal and security-focused researcher interested in frontier AI governance.

My background combines:

- legal analysis;
- security studies;
- open-source intelligence (OSINT);
- risk assessment;
- evidence-based research.

My goal is to apply these skills to emerging challenges related to advanced AI systems and their governance.

## Motivation

Frontier AI governance requires interdisciplinary approaches.

Technical capabilities alone are not sufficient to address questions about oversight, accountability and risk management.

A key challenge is translating complex technical evidence into structured information that can support institutional decisions.

## Current Work

I am the creator of the open-source project:

**Frontier Model Risk Trigger Map**

The project develops an evidence-to-governance pipeline:

Evidence  
→ Evaluation  
→ Confidence Assessment  
→ Risk Trigger  
→ Possible Governance Action

The objective is not to rank AI models or determine whether a system is safe or unsafe.

Instead, the project explores how publicly available evidence, evaluation limitations and uncertainty can be connected to proportionate governance responses.

## Research Interests

My current interests include:

- frontier AI risk governance;
- model evaluations;
- transparency and accountability mechanisms;
- AI incident reporting;
- security implications of advanced AI systems.

## Future Direction

Over the next months, I aim to:

- expand evidence coverage;
- improve independent evaluation tracking;
- develop additional governance case studies;
- strengthen connections between legal frameworks, security analysis and frontier AI governance.

## Long-Term Goal

I aim to contribute to AI safety and governance by combining legal reasoning, security analysis and reproducible open-source research.
