# Methodological Limitations

## Purpose

This document describes the main limitations of the Frontier Model Risk Trigger Map.

The project is designed to structure publicly available evidence and connect it to possible governance considerations.

It does not provide a definitive assessment of whether a frontier AI system is safe or unsafe.

---

# Public Evidence Limitations

Frontier AI evaluations are often based on information released by:

- model developers;
- research organizations;
- government institutions;
- independent evaluators.

Public information may not include:

- complete evaluation methodologies;
- internal safeguards;
- training details;
- unpublished test results.

---

# Evaluation Access Limitations

The reliability of conclusions depends on evaluation conditions.

Important factors include:

- black-box, grey-box or white-box access;
- availability of reasoning traces;
- reproducibility;
- evaluator independence;
- testing environment.

Limited access may reduce confidence in conclusions, but does not automatically imply unsafe behaviour.

---

# Benchmark Interpretation

Benchmark results should not be interpreted as direct measures of real-world risk.

Limitations include:

- differences between benchmarks;
- uncertain transfer from controlled tests to deployment;
- changing model capabilities;
- incomplete representation of real-world scenarios.

---

# Provider Threshold Limitations

Model providers may define their own:

- capability thresholds;
- risk categories;
- evaluation frameworks.

These classifications are not equivalent across providers and should not be interpreted as universal risk scales.

---

# Governance Interpretation

The project does not rank models.

Instead, it asks:

"What additional supervisory action may be justified by available evidence?"

Possible actions depend on:

- deployment context;
- access conditions;
- potential impact;
- uncertainty level.

---

# Future Improvements

Future versions may improve:

- independent evaluation coverage;
- historical trend analysis;
- incident linkage;
- cross-framework comparison;
- external review.
