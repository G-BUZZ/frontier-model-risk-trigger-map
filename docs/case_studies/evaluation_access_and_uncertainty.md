# Case Study: Evaluation Access and Uncertainty in Frontier AI Governance

## Research Question

How much confidence can policymakers place in public frontier AI evaluations when independent evaluators have limited access to models, internal testing environments or deployment information?

## Why This Matters

Frontier AI governance relies on evaluations to identify dangerous capabilities and inform oversight decisions.

However, evaluations may be affected by:

- limited model access;
- differences between black-box and white-box testing;
- incomplete visibility into safeguards;
- restricted information about training and deployment conditions.

The absence of evidence should not be interpreted as evidence of safety.

## Evidence Sources

This case study considers public evidence from:

- independent evaluation organizations;
- government AI safety institutions;
- provider-released system cards;
- governance research publications.

Relevant evidence should record:

- evaluator identity;
- access conditions;
- methodology;
- limitations;
- uncertainty.

## Observed Governance Challenge

Independent evaluators may face constraints when assessing frontier models.

Examples include:

- API-only access;
- inability to inspect internal model behaviour;
- limited information about training procedures;
- restricted access to safety testing environments.

These limitations can reduce confidence in conclusions about:

- loss-of-control risks;
- deceptive behaviour;
- advanced autonomous capabilities;
- robustness of safeguards.

## Evidence Interpretation

This framework does not assume that limited access means unsafe behaviour.

Instead, it treats limited evaluation access as an uncertainty factor affecting confidence in governance decisions.

## Example Trigger

### Trigger Condition

Public evidence indicates that:

- a model demonstrates advanced capabilities in relevant domains;
- independent evaluation access is restricted;
- available assessments rely primarily on provider-controlled information.

### Confidence Level

Medium

Reason:

The existence of capability signals may be supported by evidence, but uncertainty remains regarding internal behaviour and safeguard effectiveness.

## Possible Governance Actions

Depending on the deployment context, possible responses may include:

- expanded independent evaluation access;
- additional third-party testing;
- improved transparency requirements;
- enhanced reporting obligations;
- targeted review before high-impact deployment.

## Limitations

This case study does not determine whether a specific model is safe or unsafe.

Limitations include:

- incomplete public information;
- differences between evaluation methodologies;
- uncertainty regarding real-world deployment conditions.

## Conclusion

Frontier AI governance requires not only identifying harmful capabilities, but also understanding when uncertainty itself becomes a reason for additional oversight.

The purpose of this case study is to demonstrate an evidence-to-governance workflow that connects evaluation limitations with proportionate supervisory responses.
