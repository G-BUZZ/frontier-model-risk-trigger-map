# Case Study: Agentic Operation Risk Assessment

## Research Question

When publicly available evidence indicates increasing agentic capabilities in frontier AI models, what additional governance actions may be justified?

## Scope

This case study evaluates the relationship between:

- model capabilities;
- autonomy-related evidence;
- evaluation limitations;
- deployment exposure;
- possible supervisory responses.

This is not a safety certification or a determination that a model is unsafe.

## Evidence Collection

The assessment uses publicly available evidence from:

- frontier model system cards;
- independent evaluation reports;
- government and research organization publications.

Each evidence item is recorded with:

- source;
- date;
- evaluator type;
- access conditions;
- uncertainty level.

## Evidence Signals

### Capability Signal

Evidence of increased ability to:

- perform multi-step tasks;
- use external tools;
- maintain longer task horizons;
- operate with reduced human intervention.

### Risk Considerations

Capability alone does not determine risk.

Relevant factors include:

- deployment context;
- tool access;
- human oversight;
- evaluator access limitations;
- safeguards effectiveness.

## Evidence Interpretation

The framework does not assign a universal risk score.

Instead, it asks:

"What additional supervisory action is justified by the available evidence?"

## Example Trigger

### Trigger

Evidence suggests that a model demonstrates:

- extended autonomous task completion;
- ability to interact with external systems;
- limited independent evaluation coverage.

### Confidence

Medium

Reason:

Public evidence may indicate capability trends, but deployment context and internal evaluation details may remain unavailable.

## Possible Governance Actions

Depending on context, possible actions may include:

- additional independent evaluation;
- enhanced monitoring;
- restricted deployment in high-impact environments;
- improved incident reporting mechanisms.

## Limitations

This assessment is limited by:

- availability of public information;
- differences between evaluation methodologies;
- uncertainty about real-world deployment conditions.

## Conclusion

Frontier AI governance requires mechanisms that connect observable evidence to proportionate oversight.

The purpose of this case study is to demonstrate an evidence-to-governance workflow, not to rank models or providers.
