# Frontier Model Risk Triggers and EU AI Act Mapping

## Purpose

This document maps frontier-model risk evidence and internal trigger conditions
to relevant governance concepts under the European Union Artificial Intelligence
Act.

The mapping is not a legal opinion and does not determine regulatory status.
It provides an analytical bridge between technical evidence and possible
governance actions.

# Mapping Table

| Technical Evidence | Possible Trigger | EU AI Act Relevance | Possible Governance Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frontier model reaches significant capability threshold in cyber, bio or other high-impact domain | Capability review trigger | General-purpose AI systemic risk assessment | Additional evaluation and documentation review |
| Independent evaluation identifies unexpected harmful behaviour | External validation trigger | Risk management and monitoring processes | Replication, mitigation and reporting review |
| Significant uncertainty due to missing evaluation data | Evidence gap trigger | Transparency and documentation concerns | Request additional information |
| Harmful incident occurs after deployment | Incident trigger | Incident monitoring and reporting mechanisms | Investigation and corrective action |
| Model deployed with autonomous tools or high-impact access | Deployment-context trigger | Systemic risk depends on intended use and deployment conditions | Deployment review and safeguards assessment |

---

# Provider Responsibilities

Relevant governance expectations include:

- maintaining technical documentation;
- providing information about model capabilities and limitations;
- implementing evaluation and risk management processes;
- monitoring serious incidents and safety concerns.

---

# Oversight Perspective

Authorities and oversight bodies may require:

- clearer evidence provenance;
- independent evaluation where uncertainty is significant;
- reproducible testing methods;
- information about safeguards and deployment conditions.

---

# Limitations

This mapping does not:

- assign legal classification to specific models;
- replace conformity assessment;
- create a regulatory risk score.

Frontier model governance remains dependent on:
- model capability;
- deployment context;
- intended use;
- available evidence;
- regulatory interpretation.
